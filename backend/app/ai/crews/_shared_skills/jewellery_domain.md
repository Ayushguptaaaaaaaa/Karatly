# Skill: Jewellery trade knowledge (India) - all agents
Use this to reason like an experienced jewellery accountant. Numbers still come ONLY from the data.

## Two kinds of shop (check the design's material)
- Fine jewellery: GOLD, SILVER, PLATINUM, diamonds. High value per piece, metal price drives cost.
- Imitation / fashion jewellery (material IMITATION): AD (American Diamond = cubic zirconia stones),
  Kundan, Polki-look, oxidised (German silver), one-gram gold plated, beads, brass or copper base.
  Low price per piece (often 200 to 5,000 rupees), sold by the piece, trend driven.
- Many shops sell both. Never compare a gold design and an imitation design on price alone; compare
  margin %, pieces sold and speed of selling.

## Metals and purity
- Gold: 24K (99.9%, coins and bars), 22K (91.6%, called 916, most Indian jewellery), 18K (75%, diamond
  and modern pieces), 14K (58.5%). BIS hallmark + 6-digit HUID is mandatory on gold jewellery.
- Silver: 925 sterling (92.5%) for jewellery; 999 for coins and articles. Payal (anklets) are often silver.
- Platinum: 950 (95%). Rare, high value, slow moving.

## How fine jewellery is priced
- Price = metal weight x today's metal rate + making charges (per gram or % of metal value, often
  8 to 25%) + stone value + 3% GST. Wastage is sometimes added.
- So cost and selling price move with the gold rate. A cost jump on a reorder is often the gold rate,
  not a pricing mistake. Say so when explaining cost rises.
- Old gold exchange is common: customers trade in old jewellery against new purchases.

## Imitation jewellery business
- Fixed price per piece; margins are higher in % (often 40 to 100%+) but low in rupees.
- Fashion changes fast: a design can stop selling in weeks. Stock older than 60 to 90 days is a risk.
  Prefer small, frequent reorders over big orders. Clear old designs with offers or combos.
- Plating can tarnish in storage; old stock can lose value. Bridal and festive AD/Kundan sets sell
  before weddings and festivals, oxidised pieces sell with ethnic and casual wear.

## Taxes and billing
- GST on gold, silver, platinum and imitation jewellery is 3% (CGST 1.5% + SGST 1.5% within a state,
  IGST 3% across states). Making charges billed separately by job workers attract 5%.
- Revenue for reports = price after discount, before GST. GST is collected for the government.
- Cash purchases of 2 lakh rupees or more in a day are not allowed; PAN is needed on large bills.

## Seasons and demand (India)
- Peaks: Dhanteras and Diwali (Oct to Nov, the biggest), wedding season (Nov to Feb and Apr to May),
  Akshaya Tritiya (Apr or May, gold buying), Karwa Chauth, Raksha Bandhan, Navratri and Durga Puja,
  Onam (Kerala), Pongal (Tamil Nadu), Eid, Christmas.
- Slow: monsoon months (Jun to Aug) and Pitru Paksha / Shraddh (about 2 weeks in Sep or Oct) when
  many families avoid buying jewellery.
- Wedding buying favours necklaces, bangles, mangalsutras, sets; gifting favours rings, earrings,
  pendants and chains. Light-weight jewellery sells all year.

## Stock and dealer practice
- Dealers (suppliers / wholesalers / karigars) differ in price, making charges, finish and delivery time.
  Compare dealers on cost per piece, pieces supplied and how fast their designs sell.
- Gold stock is money: dead gold stock blocks capital but keeps metal value (it can be melted or
  remade). Dead imitation stock loses value. Treat the two differently in advice.
- Small shops keep 2 to 5 pieces of a design; big showrooms keep more. Respect the shop's usual stock.

## Customers and regions
- Repeat customers and families drive most fine-jewellery sales; thank and reward them.
- NRI customers (UAE, UK, USA, Singapore) often buy during visits and before weddings.
- Regional taste differs: South India prefers temple and antique gold; North and West prefer Kundan,
  Polki and lighter designs; Bengal likes intricate filigree work.
